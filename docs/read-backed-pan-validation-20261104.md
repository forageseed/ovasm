## Implemented behavior and target-read replay

The formal-source implementation now accepts `--target-hifi mitochondrion=PATH` (and optionally `--target-hifi plastid=PATH`) for recruited HiFi from the same individual. When unresolved configurations are enumerated, the target HiFi is aligned to the target graph separately from panel HiFi and its per-pairing read counts are reported as `target_hifi_read_counts`. With this input, ranking first compares the target short-read graph score, then the fraction of distinct candidate pairings with at least three same-individual HiFi traversals, then the existing panel heuristic and linked-walk tiebreak. The target pairing score is an uncalibrated presence heuristic: reads and pairings can be correlated, recruitment and repeats affect detection, and missing support is not proof of absence. It does not estimate a posterior or establish organelle origin; no nuclear competition is performed inside this ranking. Relative weights still describe the earlier short-read-plus-panel heuristic and explicitly exclude target HiFi and linked-walk ordering. Without `--target-hifi`, the previous ranking is retained. The input is currently used only when the short-read pipeline enumerates unresolved configurations; it is not CLR support or a general long-read assembler, and it cannot discover paths absent from the target graph. Direct reuse of the same file as both target HiFi and a prior or target short-read input is rejected by file identity, but copied files and biologically duplicated reads are not detectable from that check.

## Independent checks and sensitivity

The target graph's `u13` traversal `u6- u13- u11-` was supported by 124–362 reads per run in the eight-source panel. An independent minimap2 alignment of synthetic junction contexts gave concordant predominant support. To probe possible nuclear ambiguity, 16 exact 31-mers spanning each boundary of this u13 path were scanned against four available ZM4 nuclear haplotype assemblies. The right-boundary set had 15 matches on chr4 haplotype 1 and 14 on chr4 haplotype 4; the left-boundary set had no exact matches in any of the four assemblies. This assesses only those selected exact k-mers and assemblies, not divergent or unassembled nuclear copies, nor nuclear genomes of the eight prior samples. The apparent evidence omission arose because `phased_paths` depends on the prior sample's repeat classification; the implemented code uses per-read-deduplicated direct three-step traversals instead.

Four exploratory scoring rules (presence sum, presence mean, smoothed log and binary majority) and two junction-detection parameter settings were compared. With the default traversal data, the first three retained the held-out run's maximum observed supported-pairing count among top ties in 6/8 leave-one-run-out folds; the threshold-3 top group was unchanged for thresholds 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20 reads. These are held-out detection comparisons on a fixed target graph, not independent truth, calibration, or accuracy estimates. A within-sample read-fraction heuristic performed worse than presence on the original eight runs under the separate mean-log-fraction comparison; it was not made the default. An additional Beta-smoothed leave-one-run-out diagnostic for traversal detection at threshold 3 used 116 repeat/sample events and yielded Brier score 0.106 and log loss 0.339; events share runs and loci, and eligibility is conditioned on detecting some traversal, so these numbers do not establish topology-posterior calibration or independent replication. All definitions, fold outputs, and scripts are in the task directory.

A complementary branch-level experiment used `scripts/assembly_benchmarks/compare_pan_branch_models.py` to predict observed held-out traversal fractions, with uniform, binary-presence, per-sample mean-fraction and pooled-read-fraction predictors. With nine run labels, minimum depth 10 and 86 eligible correlated run/branch events, event-averaged cross-entropies were 0.693, 0.397, 0.390 and 0.648 respectively. For the 13 events in the ZM4-cultivar holdout alone, the corresponding cross-entropies were 0.693, 0.606, 1.019 and 1.135. At depth 10, the ZM4 holdout included three correlated u9 branch groups, with mean cross-entropies 1.128, 1.719 and 2.279 for binary-presence, per-sample fraction and pooled-read fraction respectively. It included no u10 group: all four ZM4 u10 branch groups had substantial detected holdout reads, but zero of the other eight runs met the minimum training-depth threshold for any of those groups. Excluded group names, depths and reasons are now explicit in the tool output, so this experiment does not assess u10 predictive calibration. The per-sample fraction model therefore performed slightly better in the pooled event average but worse on that holdout; its advantage in the event average and its worse ZM4 holdout also persisted at minimum depths 30 and 100. Results depend on candidate-derived groups, fixed graph, pseudocount, depth and detected reads. Run/group events share reads and runs may be related, so they are not 86 independent observations. These are scores for observed traversal fractions, not a calibrated distribution over physical configurations. Raw count vectors were checked against all nine source evidence reports; formal outputs are `branch-predictive-formal-depth{10,30,100}.json`. The implementation reports categorical Brier scores separately from squared errors of the observed fractions. At minimum depth 10, increasing the symmetric pseudocount from 0.5 to 2 and 10 changed the ZM4-holdout cross-entropy from 0.606 to 0.567 and 0.617 for binary presence, and from 1.019 to 0.853 and 0.641 for per-sample mean fractions. A further prior-strength sensitivity experiment formed a conditional Beta prior `Beta(0.5 + k*p, 0.5 + k*(1-p))` for each binary graph branch, where `p` was the mean of smoothed traversal fractions from leave-one-run-out panel runs; it then updated this prior with randomly sampled target-run traversal counts and evaluated the remaining counts. Across the same 86 eligible events and 100 seeded resamples, no target counts gave ZM4 mean cross-entropy 0.693 for `k=0`, 0.642 for `k=2`, and 0.792 for `k=10`. With 10 sampled target traversals, ZM4 cross-entropy was 0.228 for `k=0`, 0.255 for `k=2`, and 0.353 for `k=10`. This is simulated splitting of aggregate observations from the same run, not a split by read IDs, independent biological replication, calibrated posterior, or topology truth. The branch groups share molecules and may be correlated; `k` is an arbitrary sensitivity parameter. It shows why a strong pan prior can harm this target and is not justification for a default biological probability. Source and all settings: `evaluate-panel-pseudocount-target-splits.py` and `panel-pseudocount-target-splits.json`.

The standalone experimental `scripts/assembly_benchmarks/experimental_branch_posterior.py` makes the conditional prior/update explicit for a candidate-derived branch. It uses an empirical mean of donor-run traversal fractions only when at least two donors meet the depth threshold, a base 0.5 concentration per alternative, and user-specified prior strength. The target is excluded from donors. `--target-effective-cap 0` uses all target traversal counts under an explicit iid multinomial working model; a positive cap rescales counts and is a fractional-likelihood generalized posterior. Counts for missing alternatives are not silently treated as zero, and donor runs missing an alternative are excluded. These are model-conditional traversal-fraction distributions with model-based variance, **not** calibrated topology probabilities, independent molecule frequencies, or certified origins. For ZM4 group `u2- u15-`, target HiFi traversal counts are 11 versus 1,577; three eligible panel runs have a mean fraction 0.932 for the first alternative. With an arbitrary effective target cap of 10, prior strengths 0, 2 and 10 yield first-alternative conditional means 0.052, 0.187 and 0.471 respectively. This illustrates sensitivity to the panel and cap; the resampling experiment above does not validate those numerical probabilities. Source, six targeted tests, source hash and complete results: `experimental_branch_posterior.py`, `tests/test_experimental_branch_posterior.py`, `experimental-branch-posterior-v2-source.sha256`, and `zm4-branch-posterior-v2-k{0,2,10}-cap10.json`.

## Low-coverage leave-one-run-out simulation

A separate diagnostic sampled idealized 150-bp paired fragments from the first up-to-2,500 recruited HiFi reads per organelle, 15 fragments per source read and approximately 320-bp insert sizes. No additional Illumina error was simulated; the original HiFi base errors remain. For each of eight runs, its source P paths were removed from both organelle reference graphs, reference nodes and edges were restricted to the remaining seven runs' paths, and its HiFi read files were excluded from the prior. The held-out run's HiFi reads were then mapped to the resulting target graph. Because the simulated reads originate in those very long molecules, this is an internal reconstruction stress test, not independent ground truth.

The pre-guard and guard-enabled source snapshots were compared on all eight inputs. For SRR31693262, selected graph bases increased from 4,747 (k=127) to 289,603 (k=55), and HiFi reads supporting at least one graph junction increased from 387 to 3,493. For SRR31693266, graph bases increased from 8,931 (k=127) to 226,854 (k=55), with HiFi-supporting reads increasing from 344 to 1,771 of 2,278. On the larger graphs the held-out HiFi evidence supported 85/98 and 44/48 graph links respectively; unsupported links require separate interpretation and are not automatically errors. In the other six runs, selected graph bases and HiFi-supporting read counts were unchanged. The larger graph and more supported junctions do not prove complete organelle sequence, correct origin, or unique topology.
